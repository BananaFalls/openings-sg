## What was done, in order

1. **QA review of the stack.** Back end tests (133), type check and lint all passed; the live site and API answered normally. Found one real risk and several visible data problems, listed below.
2. **Partial scrapes no longer shrink the site.** The stats count a posting as live only if the latest run saw it, so a run that died part way used to refresh the site with only what it reached. The scraper now refreshes only after a whole `--all` crawl; otherwise the site keeps the last good numbers.
3. **Role names cleaned up** (`role_of()` in the scraper). Agency job codes ("6723 - Admin Assistant" was the role "6723", 293 postings) are skipped; "Team Lead", "Head of Sales", "Lead Generation Specialist" and a bare "Principal" are kept; emoji and `#HKH`-style tags are dropped; all-caps words like "CHEF" are title-cased; "and", "of" and similar stay lower case. On 4,000 real titles: "Other" and code-named roles went to zero, one-off roles fell about 15%. Takes effect as each nightly scrape rewrites the live jobs. `role_of()` no longer matches `roleOf()` in the old `web/index.html`.
4. **Pay ceiling.** A midpoint over S$50,000 a month is treated as missing, like the existing S$300 floor. Above that it was nearly all yearly pay typed as monthly (e.g. "Global IT", S$257,500 a month). The generated column is swapped only when its rule differs, so `db/schema.sql` stays safe to re-run.
5. **Honest labels.** Role pages said "50 companies hiring" when 519 were; now "The 50 with the most postings, of 519". Sector pages now say "Show the top 200 roles" when the API's 200 cap is reached.
6. **Pay by experience** (new). Each sector page shows advertised pay for Junior, Mid-level and Senior, by the least years of experience a posting asks for. Pressing a level lists the roles offered at that level, sortable by median pay or by postings, linking to the role pages.
   - Data: MCF's `minimumYearsExperience`, which only the per-job details endpoint has. New column `jobs.min_experience`, function `set_details()` (replaces `set_vacancies()`), views `stats_sector_exp` and `stats_sector_exp_role`, levels defined once in the view `exp_levels`.
   - API: `/v1/sectors/{slug}` gains `experience` (level, years, postings, pay, roles).
   - Levels were chosen from a sample of 2,461 public postings (60 per sector): 63% asked for 0 to 2 years, 29% for 3 to 5, 8.5% for 6 or more. Median pay S$3,500 / S$5,250 / S$9,500. Pay was flat from 0 to 2 years, so "no experience" is not a level of its own.
   - A level shows pay only when at least 5 postings state it; the page says how many postings it rests on and that they are a sample.
7. **Repo made public** so Actions minutes are free. History was checked first: no keys, passwords or personal email in any commit.
8. **Nightly lookups switched on:** 3,500 a night, newest postings first. The run now refreshes the site and saves the history straight after the crawl, then does the lookups, then refreshes once more. A lookup failure can no longer delay the site or cost a day of history.
9. **Engineering and IT prioritised.** New `--details-only` and `--details-sectors` scraper options and the "Experience lookups" workflow. Started 12:12 SGT for both sectors side by side (IT all 5,862, Engineering 6,500 of 7,217). The stats refresh after every 1,000 postings stored.
10. **Lookups reuse one connection.** Each lookup used to open a new secure connection, several round trips from GitHub's US servers to Singapore. Applies from the 14 Oct night run.
11. **Other job sources checked.** None beats MCF. Adzuna and Careerjet have free APIs and Singapore sites but repeat other boards and lack experience; JobStreet (SEEK) and LinkedIn give no read access; Careers@Gov has no API. data.gov.sg has official MOM vacancy totals by industry under an open licence.

## Decisions and why

- **Three levels: 0 to 2, 3 to 5, 6+ years.** They match what employers ask for (almost every posting names 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 or 10 years) and where pay steps up. Splitting Senior at 10 leaves too few postings per sector.
- **Experience is a sample, and says so.** One request per posting makes full coverage of all 71,000 impossible each night, so only looked-up postings count.
- **3,500 lookups a night, repo public.** About a day's new postings. A private repo's 2,000 free minutes would have allowed only about 400.
- **Ceiling at S$50,000, on the midpoint.** It keeps genuine top pay (directors, specialist doctors) and drops the absurd. One genuine private-banking posting (S$50k to S$85k) is also lost; accepted.
- **A Supabase read with the service key was blocked** by Claude Code's permissions. Role and pay analysis used public MCF data instead. Reading with the service key needs the founder's approval each time, or the founder runs the command.

## How to change things from here

- **Levels:** edit `exp_levels` in `db/schema.sql` and run the file in Supabase. The front end reads the level names and years from the API.
- **Ceiling:** change `50000` in both places in the "pay rule" block of `db/schema.sql`, and the note in `CLAUDE.md`.
- **More sectors first:** Actions, then Experience lookups, then Run workflow, with sector slugs (the part of the sector page address after `/sectors/`).
- **Seeing new data on the site:** sector and role pages rebuild once a day. A push to `main` rebuilds them at once, after the API's one-hour cache has caught up.
